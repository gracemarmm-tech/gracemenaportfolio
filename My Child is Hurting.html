Project Information

    • Project: Am I Impure for Growing?
    • Category: Dance Videography
    • Dance: Improvisation
    • Concept: Body, Shame, Growth & Purity
    • Performer: Grace Mena
    • Year: 2024

Project Overview

Am I Impure for Growing? is a dance piece about learning to look at and inhabit my own body without shame. Growing up, I developed a sense of guilt around my body. I saw it as something impure, and even watching it move made me uncomfortable. For a long time, I learned to ignore it rather than experience it.

Through this piece, I wanted to do the opposite: to look at my body in motion, allow it to be seen, and learn to appreciate it simply for the way it exists and moves. Showing my body initially felt like an act of impurity, even when there was nothing inherently impure about what I was doing.

The piece also reflects on my difficulty accepting that growing meant my body would change and that with those changes, people might begin to make different assumptions about me. It questions why growing into one’s body can alter the way purity is perceived and asks: Am I impure simply for growing?

Creative Process

Written Reflection

My inner child was told that her body could cause a man to sin, and that confused her.

Without realizing it, I began attaching impurity, guilt, and shame to my body. At the same time, it made me feel somehow superior to men. I do not say that with pride, but with frustration.

Are women supposed to protect men from their own attraction to us because it is apparently so powerful that they cannot control themselves? If that's true, how powerful we must be, and poor little creatures. (It isn't. I'm making fun of the people who say this.)

I originally approached this piece with a much more spiritual message, but then I found myself amused by how afraid I was to share the video and by how much I still fear having impurity projected onto me.

My inner child hurts when the purity she was once given is taken away from her simply because she grew.

If someone watches this video and sees impurity in me, perhaps the impurity belongs to the way they are looking at me.

Editing Process

The editing process became another form of experimentation. I began layering different clips I had recorded at the beach, playing with transparency, movement, and overlapping images to visually recreate the energy I felt while dancing, especially the energy I felt moving through my hands.

I also created a digital drawing in Procreate and incorporated it at the beginning of the video to give another visual dimension to the feeling behind the piece.

Rather than following a fixed plan, I allowed myself to experiment with the footage, combine different elements, and see what they became together.

Creating this piece felt like playing. Editing became a space where I could explore freely, layering movement, drawings, textures, and images until they began to reflect what I had felt in my body.
